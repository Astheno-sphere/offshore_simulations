# Study: two abhinavbwj reels, "The City draws itself" (Hamburg, DbbScA7Icv1, 2026-07-30) and "The Pol, Ahmedabad" (DbdfwNLogtW, 2026-07-31)

Method: both viewed in the owner's Chrome, muted, 5 zoomed samples each at roughly 0, 3, 7, 12 and 17 s after load, plus page text. No download, nothing committed from the images.
What this can and cannot give: the states and their order, layout, type, colour and annotation style. It cannot give shot lengths, easing, camera speed, cut types or sound sync. Those need the video file (see "Next").
Audio credit shown on the posts: Hamburg uses a track titled "floating signal", Pol uses Ryoji Ikeda "data.matrix". Both are in-app audio tied to the data reveals; not usable outside Instagram.

## Reel 1: Hamburg, "The City draws itself" (9:16)
- States seen in order: (1) a rotating sphere-like node graph in colour clusters (probably the thumbnail or a loop point); (2) a dark map where orange dots (journeys) accumulate, three counters at top-left that tick up, title in handwritten-script capitals; (3) street network drawn in cyan, white and magenta glow with labelled layer tags down the right edge ("traffic exposure", "walk doorstep", "agency accumulation", "sun and shadow", "green access") each with a small dot and leader; (4) an exploded layer stack titled "five ways to read a neighbourhood": built density as small isometric blocks, green and public space, movement network, an urban intensity field as contour lines, and the built form, each layer a labelled slab; (5) "one neighbourhood as one data volume": a 3D point cube with a colour height scale bar in the corner.
- Device: the city "draws itself" means layers are built up one by one on a locked map, then lifted apart in 3D, then fused into one volume. The journey is from flat map to stacked layers to data volume.
- Type: script-style title (distinct from the bold sans of the carousels), small spaced capitals for chapter strapline, mono-like counters, thin leader lines to labels. Handle bottom centre. Every layer has a colour of its own on black.

## Reel 2: The Pol, Ahmedabad (9:16)
- Loop of states on one place: (1) satellite image with the old-city street network highlighted in yellow-white at the centre, title top-left in bold white sans, two lines; (2) the whole frame filled by a dense neon street network over land-use coloured in dark green and rust, the old core burning white at the centre; (3) the camera tips into an oblique perspective of the same data, grey ground, same neon lines, green park blocks standing up; (4) all colour stripped away to black with only the street network in thin white and blue lines and a small data caption ("isovist, street network, km squared"); (5) back to satellite with the yellow network over the whole city, then the loop.
- Device: one place, one dataset, restyled five ways. Colour is removed to show structure and added back to show land use. The camera move is one tilt from top-down to oblique, the only perspective change in the sample.
- Type: title top-left, no counters, a tiny caption; data decides everything else. 37 comments and 55 reposts for a reel with no text explanation; the top comment asks how it was made.

## What both add to the definitions
1. A film can be one dataset shown in a sequence of restyled states: satellite, full colour, oblique, stripped, back. Change of style, not change of scene, drives interest.
2. Layer build-up then separation ("explode") then fusion is a reusable narrative for any multi-layer model: we can show land, sea, weather, plan, replay as layers.
3. Counters in a corner that tick as the map fills turn a map into a measurement; each counter has a small caption.
4. Annotation by leader lines with a dot, small capital labels, no boxes.
5. A colour scale bar appears only in the data-volume state, where colour means a number.
6. Audio is part of the product for him (data-driven, minimal electronic). Ours is silent-first; if audio is added it must be unlicensed-free and tied to events.
7. Honest limit: I did not see a neural-network simulation in any of the five Abhinav posts studied. The simulations are classical numerical solvers (advection, diffusion, rasterised masks) and network analyses.
